A hike over the ridge.

Follow Bull Creek north, then climb into the preserve. The connected City trail map holds about 12.9 miles of trails, but its farthest road exit is 5.0 miles from Lakewood. A 15-mile day needs side spurs and retracing; it is not a single through-hike.

01

Check the water

The USGS gauge at Loop 360 gives creek context. It does not measure the depth or footing at either ford, and no crossing threshold has been established here.

02

Know the preserve rules

Forest Ridge allows hikers in groups of 10 or fewer. No dogs, bikes, horses, camping or campfires. Stay on the authorized trail; gates may close temporarily.

Forest Ridge: OPEN when checked Oct. 21, 2026. This page does not fetch live trail status.

03

Pack for the ridge

Expect rocky climbs and uneven limestone. Carry your water: the City lists no running water or restrooms in Forest Ridge. Bring footwear with grip, sun protection and a light if your return may run late.

Download the GPX into a hiking app before leaving. This page’s basemap needs a connection; the GPX contains a calculated route, not a recorded hike.

Sources, route method & what remains unchecked

City trail geometry was captured September 29, 2026; OpenStreetMap creek geometry September 28. Distances come from a calculated route along those lines, with trail vertices within two metres joined. The start is approximate; parking approaches have not been walked.

The longer option ends at the farthest City-mapped St. Edward’s access point in the connected network, not a surveyed parking bay. The shorter option ends at the preserve-to-park connection on the ridge. The 12.9-mile network figure counts branches once; it is not a prebuilt 12.9- or 15-mile route. The site marks only the road exits supported by the route data; verify a safe, legal pickup point on the ground.

Possible fords are geometric intersections, not confirmed crossing structures. The long tour adds a third candidate on a St. Edward’s branch and revisits crossing locations. Minor tributaries, gullies, washouts, current road conditions, trail signs and current passage have not been surveyed. Follow signs on the ground. Do not use a road detour around high water based on this map.

Time estimates assume two miles an hour plus 30–60 minutes of stops; your pace and creek conditions can change that considerably. Trail status is a dated check, not a live feed.
